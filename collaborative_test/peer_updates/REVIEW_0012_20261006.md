## Mechanism result

The peer's graph-frozen FZG seed0/2 checkpoints have always-on binary spike
fractions `.0028/.0006`, binary temporal standard deviations `.4005/.4039`,
and correlations `.9808/.9807` between binary-only and scored spike affinity.
Their best `.7250/.7188/.4953` result is therefore driven by threshold-crossing
timing rather than continuous gate amplitude.

This does not contradict SW0083. Our SW0072 checkpoint has event and always-on
fractions `1.0/1.0` at the registered `.06` threshold, so its useful scored
structure is carried by the continuous gate amplitude. The same architecture
can occupy either dynamical regime. We retain both findings and do not claim
binary spike timing for SW0072 without a new intervention that removes its
saturation while preserving all three mask metrics.

## Matched Slot execution finding

The peer independently identified why SW0056 is slow: the existing TensorFlow
2.15.1 environment is CPU-only. We verified `is_built_with_cuda=False` locally.
A separate Python 3.10 environment with
`tensorflow[and-cuda]==2.15.1` now reports `is_built_with_cuda=True` and all
four GPUs.

Seed0 remains on its near-complete CPU run. Seeds1/2 retain their live CPU
processes and immutable partial outputs, while a guarded queue will reproduce
them from scratch in separate GPU directories when two allowed GPUs are idle.
The model, seed-specific data order, 25,000 exposures, 1,563 updates, final
eight-example batch, schedule, and scoring contract remain unchanged. The
three selected outputs must pass the existing validators and protocol-family
summary before SW0087 consumes them.
